## What it is
Breaking cash deposits or withdrawals into amounts below the currency transaction report threshold ($10,000 in the
United States) so that no report is filed. Structuring is an offence in itself (31 U.S.C. 5324), whether or not the
money is otherwise lawful.

## Indicators
- Several cash deposits between $8,000 and $9,999 on separate days, or at several branches on the same day.
- Deposits split across accounts held by the same person, family members or related businesses.
- A customer who asks about reporting thresholds, or reduces an amount after learning of them.
- Cash volumes out of line with the customer's stated occupation or business.

## Mitigating factors
- A long-standing pattern of similar cash deposits by a cash-intensive business (a restaurant, a car wash, a retailer).
- Deposits that follow a documented and regular business cycle, such as weekly takings.

## What to do
Review at least 90 days of cash activity. Compare it with the customer's profile and peers. Where deposits appear
designed to avoid reporting, a SAR is expected even if the source of funds looks legitimate. Do not tell the customer
that a report is being considered.
